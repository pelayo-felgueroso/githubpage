## Explanation

Kinematics is about understanding how a robot moves. It describes how the position and orientation of its different parts change when its joints move, without looking at the forces involved.

There are two main concepts: [[Forward Kinematics]], which calculates where the robot's end effector is based on its joint positions, and [[Inverse Kinematics]], which does the opposite by finding the joint positions needed to reach a target.

To work with robot movements, we also use [[Coordinate Frames]] and [[Transformations]] to describe positions and orientations relative to different parts of the robot. The [[Jacobian Matrix]] helps us understand how joint movements affect the movement of the end effector and is also useful for identifying [[Robot Singularities]].

These concepts are used to calculate and understand robot movements, especially when a robot needs to reach a specific position.
