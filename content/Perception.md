## Explanation

Perception is the process through which a robot or computer system collects and interprets information about its environment.

In robotics, perception usually relies on sensors such as cameras, depth cameras, LiDAR and other measurement devices. The collected data is processed to identify objects, estimate their positions and understand what is happening around the robot.

Computer vision is an important part of robotic perception, but perception can also combine information from several different sensors. For example, a robot may use a camera to identify an object, a depth sensor to estimate its distance and an encoder to know the current position of its own joints.

The output of a perception system can be used by other parts of the robot. A motion planning system, for example, needs information about objects and obstacles in the environment to calculate a safe movement.

Perception is therefore not just about detecting objects. It is about turning sensor measurements into information that a robot can use to make decisions and interact with the physical world.
