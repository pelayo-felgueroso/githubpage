## Explanation

Specular Reflection occurs when light reflects from a smooth or shiny surface, producing highlights that depend on the position of the light source and the viewing direction.

For example, when looking at a polished metal object or a glass surface, we can often see bright reflections of nearby lights or objects.

Unlike [[Lambertian Reflection]], where light is scattered equally in all viewing directions, specular reflection concentrates reflected light around a particular direction. This is why the appearance of shiny objects can change significantly when either the object or the camera moves.

The Phong Reflection Model is one way of approximating this behaviour. It combines ambient, diffuse and specular components to describe the appearance of a surface.

In Computer Vision, specular reflections can make object detection, surface reconstruction and measurements more difficult because highlights may change depending on the viewing and lighting conditions.
