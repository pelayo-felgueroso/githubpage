## Explanation

A Camera Obscura is an optical device that projects an image of the outside world onto a surface inside a dark room or box. It consists of a closed space with a small opening that allows light to enter.

Light travels in straight lines through the opening. Rays coming from the top of an object reach the bottom of the projection surface, while rays coming from the bottom reach the top. As a result, the projected image appears upside down and reversed.

The Camera Obscura is one of the earliest examples of image formation and helped establish the principles behind photography and modern cameras.

The same basic principle is used in the [[Pinhole Camera Model]], which describes how a 3D scene is projected onto a 2D image plane.
