## Explanation

Robot links are the rigid structural components that connect the joints of a robot. Together with the joints, they form the mechanical structure of a robot arm.

Each link has physical properties such as length, mass, shape and inertia. These properties influence the robot's movement, payload capacity and dynamic behaviour.

The arrangement of links and joints determines the robot's kinematic structure and defines its reachable workspace.

For example, a simple robot arm may consist of several links connected by revolute joints. By rotating the joints, the robot can position its [[End Effector]] in different locations.

Links are usually represented in a robot model using geometric and physical properties. This information is needed by simulation and motion planning software to understand the robot's structure and check for collisions.
