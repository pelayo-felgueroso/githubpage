## Explanation

RGB is a colour model that represents colours by combining three primary colour channels: Red, Green and Blue.

Each channel stores an intensity value. In a typical 8-bit image, each channel ranges from 0 to 255, giving approximately 16.7 million possible colour combinations.

For example, a high red value combined with low green and blue values produces a red colour. When all three channels have the same value, the result is a shade of grey. When all channels are at their maximum value, the result is white.

RGB is commonly used by digital cameras, displays and computer vision systems. However, it is not always the most convenient colour representation for image processing. Depending on the task, an image can be converted into other colour spaces such as grayscale, HSV or Lab.

For example, HSV can make it easier to separate colours based on their hue, while grayscale removes colour information and keeps only intensity.
