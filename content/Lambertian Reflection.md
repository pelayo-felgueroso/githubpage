## Explanation

Lambertian Reflection is a simple model that describes how light reflects from a perfectly diffuse surface, such as a matte wall.

When light hits a Lambertian surface, it is scattered equally in all viewing directions. This means that the observed brightness does not depend on where the camera is positioned, but on the orientation of the surface relative to the incoming light.

The brightness also depends on the surface's reflectance, called [[Albedo]], which describes how much light the material reflects.

Real surfaces are rarely perfectly Lambertian, but this model is useful because it simplifies how we describe light and shading in images.

In Computer Vision, Lambertian reflection is often used when studying illumination, surface appearance and 3D shape estimation.
