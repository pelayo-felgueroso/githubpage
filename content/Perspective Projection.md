## Explanation

Perspective Projection describes how a 3D scene is represented in a 2D image. It is the type of projection used by most conventional cameras.

Light rays travel from points in the scene through the camera's optical centre and reach the image plane. Because of this geometry, objects appear smaller as their distance from the camera increases. This is why a nearby object looks larger than an identical object farther away.

The relationship between a 3D point and its position in the image depends on its distance from the camera and the camera's focal length.

Perspective projection is important in Computer Vision because it allows us to understand how real-world positions are represented in images and is used in tasks such as [[Camera Calibration]], [[Pose Estimation]] and 3D reconstruction.
