## Explanation

Albedo describes how much light a surface reflects compared to the amount of light it receives. It is a property of the material and is usually represented as a value between 0 and 1.

A surface with a high albedo reflects a large proportion of incoming light, while a surface with a low albedo absorbs more light.

For example, a white surface generally has a higher albedo than a black surface. This does not mean that a white surface always appears brighter in an image, because the observed brightness also depends on the illumination and the orientation of the surface.

In Computer Vision, albedo is useful for separating the appearance of a material from the effects of lighting. This is particularly relevant when analysing surface properties and estimating the 3D shape of objects.
