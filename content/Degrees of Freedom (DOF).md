## Explanation

Degrees of Freedom (DOF) describe the number of independent movements or variables needed to define the position and configuration of a system.

In robotics, each independent joint movement usually contributes one degree of freedom. For example, a robot arm with six independent revolute joints has six degrees of freedom.

A rigid body moving freely in three-dimensional space has six degrees of freedom: three translations along the X, Y and Z axes, and three rotations around those axes.

The number of degrees of freedom determines how many different movements a robot can perform and how flexibly it can position its end effector.

[[Grübler's Formula]] is used to calculate the number of degrees of freedom of a mechanical mechanism.
